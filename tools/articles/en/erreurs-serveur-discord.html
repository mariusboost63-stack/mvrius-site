A Discord server can attract hundreds of new members… and lose them just as fast. Often, the problem isn't the community's topic but a few organisational mistakes that are easy to fix. Here are the five I come across most often when I take over a server.

1. No welcome at all

A new member arrives, sees a long list of channels and doesn't know where to start. The result: they leave without writing a single message.

Set up a clear welcome channel, a greeting message and an obvious path: read the rules, pick roles, introduce yourself. The first few minutes often decide everything.

2. Too many channels

One channel per topic sounds like a good idea… until every one of them is empty. A conversation scattered across twenty channels makes a server feel dead, even when it has plenty of members.

Start with few channels, and only create a new one when a discussion genuinely outgrows its original channel.

3. Vague rules, or rules applied case by case

Rules that are too long don't get read, and rules that are too vague protect no one. And rules applied differently depending on the member quickly create a sense of unfairness.

Write short, concrete rules, and define a scale of sanctions known to the whole moderation team.

4. Moderation that is absent… or too strict

Without moderation, conflicts settle in and the calmest members are the first to leave. On the other hand, a team that punishes everything stifles the conversation.

The right balance: a team that is present, explains before sanctioning and steps in quickly on real misbehaviour.

5. No reason to come back

A server where nothing happens ends up forgotten, even if it is perfectly organised.

Create regular moments: an event every week, a contest every month, a debate, a new feature announcement. They are what turn visitors into regulars.

In short

Welcome, simplify, supervise, engage: these are the four pillars of a community that lasts. If your server doesn't tick all these boxes yet, take the quick check-up or let's talk about it directly.
