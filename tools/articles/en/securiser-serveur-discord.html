A raid, a wave of spam or a hacked staff account can damage a server in minutes. The good news: most of these incidents can be prevented with simple settings, done once and for all. Here are the ones I check first when I take over a server.

The verification level

In the server's safety settings, the verification level filters which accounts can post. For a public server, the “Medium” level is a good starting point: it requires a verified email address and a Discord account created more than five minutes ago, which blocks a large share of throwaway accounts. During an attack, raise it one notch temporarily.

AutoMod, the moderator that never sleeps

Discord's built-in AutoMod blocks messages before they are even posted. At the very least, turn on:

  • The spam filter: suspicious messages are blocked automatically.
  • The mention limit: a message that mentions too many members at once is blocked, and its author can be timed out.
  • A list of banned words: insults, common scam links, invites to other servers.

Send the alerts to a staff-only channel: the team sees what was blocked and can react.

Permissions that fit

The “Administrator” permission grants every right, without exception. Keep it for yourself, and give each staff role only what it needs. Check the @everyone role too: members shouldn't be able to mention the whole server, create invites at will or manage webhooks. Finally, put the roles in the right order: a role can only act on the roles placed below it.

2FA for the whole team

A hacked moderator account is one of the worst possible weak spots. Discord lets you require two-factor authentication for moderation actions: turn it on, and ask every staff member to set it up on their account.

Bots, with care

A bot often has more power than a moderator. Only add well-known, useful bots, remove the ones you no longer use, and refuse permissions they ask for without a reason. A well-configured security bot complements AutoMod: raid protection, action logs, verification of new members.

Plan ahead for a raid

When it happens, you need to act fast. Decide in advance who does what: pause invites, turn on slow mode in the affected channels, raise the verification level, then ban the accounts involved and clean up the messages. The server's audit log then helps you understand what happened.
