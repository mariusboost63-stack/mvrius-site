A growing community can't rely on a single person. Past a certain number of members, a moderation team becomes essential: for safety, for the atmosphere and for your own peace of mind. Here is how I recruit and train my teams.

Knowing when to recruit

The signs are clear: problematic messages that stay up for hours, conflicts that escalate, questions left unanswered. It's better to recruit a little too early than too late, because training someone takes time.

Define the roles before looking for people

  • Moderation: enforce the rules and handle conflicts.
  • Events: organise activities and keep the channels lively.
  • Support: answer questions and handle tickets.

Clear roles stop everyone from doing everything… or nobody from doing anything.

A simple recruitment process

  1. A written application: availability, experience, motivation. It already shows how serious the candidate is.
  2. A voice chat: to check their judgement, their calm and the way they communicate.
  3. A trial period: a few weeks with limited permissions, before confirming the role.

Train, then equip

A new moderator needs guidelines: a moderation charter, a shared scale of sanctions and examples of situations the team has already faced. On the tools side, set up a staff-only channel, logs to keep track of actions, and bots to automate the most repetitive tasks.

Keeping a team motivated

A moderation team often works behind the scenes. Recognise their work, hold regular check-ins, share the workload and listen to their feedback: that's what makes the difference between a team that lasts six months and one that lasts for years.
