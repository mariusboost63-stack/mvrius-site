The rules are often the first channel a new member sees… and the one they skim the fastest. Yet they set the tone of the server and protect your moderation team. Good rules aren't long: they are clear, known by everyone and applied the same way to each member.

Short, concrete, readable

Beyond about ten rules, nobody reads any more. Number them, write one sentence per rule and favour examples over grand principles: “no advertising for another server, even in private messages” is more useful than “be respectful of the community”.

The essential rules

  • Respect: no insults, harassment or discrimination.
  • No spam or advertising: repeated messages, unsolicited links, invites to other servers.
  • Appropriate content: nothing shocking or explicit outside the channels meant for it, if there are any.
  • Privacy: don't share other people's personal information.
  • The right topic in the right place: every channel has its purpose.
  • Discord's terms: they apply on your server too, minimum age included.

Then add the rules specific to your community: spoilers, self-promotion for creators, trades between members…

Announce sanctions in advance

A surprise sanction always feels unfair. State in the rules the scale you apply: a warning, then a timeout, then a kick, and finally a ban. Also make it clear that serious cases (threats, illegal content, raids) lead straight to a ban.

Have them accepted on arrival

Rules nobody has opened are useless. On a Community server, Discord can show the rules to new members and ask them to accept before they can write. Otherwise, only give the “Member” role after a click on a button or a reaction under the rules: the chat channels stay hidden until then.

Apply the same rules to everyone

This is the point that does the most damage when it's neglected. One friend of the staff who is never sanctioned, and the whole credibility of the rules collapses. Get your team to agree on how each rule is interpreted, and keep a record of sanctions to stay consistent over time.

Keep them up to date

Rules are never set in stone. When a new situation comes up several times, add a rule; when a rule no longer serves a purpose, remove it. Announce every change in the announcements channel, with the update date at the bottom of the rules.

In short
